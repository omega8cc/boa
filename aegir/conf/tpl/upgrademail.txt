Hello,

We are pleased to inform you that your Ægir instance has been successfully upgraded to [boa.version]

@=> A whole new home for the documentation

Everything you can do in your Ægir control panel and your account's shell is now documented in one place: https://docs.boa.io -- a brand-new, unified documentation site with guides organised around the work you actually do, quick-start cheat sheets, and a complete reference. And whenever a new release lands, it is announced with substance at https://omega8.cc/news -- worth a bookmark, or grab the RSS feed.

@=> Backdrop CMS, welcome aboard

Your control panel now speaks a second language: Backdrop CMS, the community continuation of the Drupal 7 lineage. Backdrop platforms appear alongside your Drupal ones with their own clear badge, and Backdrop sites install, clone, back up, restore and renew their HTTPS certificates through exactly the same one-click tasks you already know. If you look after Drupal 6 or 7 sites that need a long-term future, there is now a complete upgrade path you can drive from the panel: a task that converts a copy of a Drupal 7 site to Backdrop while the original keeps serving untouched, a companion task that lifts a Drupal 6 site to Drupal 7 first, and a cutover task that hands the original address over to the new copy the moment you are happy with it. Your site keeps its URL, its content, and its home -- it simply gains a future.

@=> Smoother, more dependable modern Drupal

Drupal 10 and 11 sites now come out of clones, migrations and fresh installs in a reliably consistent state -- a subtle class of cache poisoning that could leave a freshly deployed site confused until its next Verify is gone for good. A failed install now says so clearly instead of pretending everything went fine, and the one-time login link on a fresh site works every time. Renaming a site between its www and bare-domain form is now a single Migrate step, and imported sites arrive enabled and ready instead of parked in a queue.

@=> A sharper shield, with fewer moving parts for you

The abuse guard around your sites learned several new tricks: it now recognises scanner fleets that spread their probing across many addresses to stay under the radar, refuses requests for admin pages that simply cannot exist on your sites, and extends its automatic protection to IPv6 traffic. None of it needs anything from you -- your sites are simply harder to bother.

@=> A note for CiviCRM and Elysia cron users

This release also hardens how BOA's command-line layer loads background (Drush) extensions -- only trusted locations are used now, closing a long-standing attack path. CiviCRM and Elysia cron background tasks keep working once a small server-side opt-in is enabled by your host or server admin -- web pages are unaffected either way. If you rely on either of these and notice anything unusual about scheduled tasks after this upgrade, just ask your host or server admin to enable that opt-in.

@=> Develop locally, deploy confidently

If you like working on your sites locally, the new ddev-boa add-on pulls a copy of your site -- database and files -- straight into a local DDEV project through your own account's shell access. Develop and experiment locally, then deploy through the panel as usual.
