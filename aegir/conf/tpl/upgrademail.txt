Hello,

We are pleased to inform you that your Ægir instance has been successfully upgraded to [boa.version]

This is the Griffin release, the largest BOA has ever shipped, and a surprising amount of it shows up right where you work: in your control panel and in what your visitors feel. One thing you will notice first: this upgrade queues a single automatic Verify for each of your Drupal and Backdrop sites so they pick up the new server settings, and as tasks now run one after another, your own tasks wait their turn behind them. Here is the tour.

----------------------------------------
TWO MORE SYSTEMS, ONE CONTROL PANEL
----------------------------------------

Grav 2 and Textpattern now live in the same Ægir control panel as your Drupal and Backdrop sites, with the tasks you already know: install, verify, backup, restore, clone and upgrade. Grav is a fast flat-file system that needs no database at all, so a Grav site is as light as a website gets; Textpattern is a long-loved publishing system with a loyal following. Both can be upgraded on a copy first, so you test the new version beside the live one and switch it over only when you are happy. Where your server administrator has them switched on, their platforms are built along with the others your account's list of platforms asks for: ALL includes them, and a list of your own needs GRV and TXP added. You do not have to wait for the next scheduled upgrade: the README.txt in your account's static/control folder shows how an empty run-upgrade.pid beside platforms.info starts your instance upgrade within a few minutes.

----------------------------------------
A SITE IN A FOLDER OF YOUR DOMAIN
----------------------------------------

A Drupal or Backdrop site can now live under a path of one of your domains, such as example.com/blog, and it gets everything a site of its own gets: its own files and images, its own cron and database updates, even its own PHP version. Add it in the panel with the address including the path; the main domain may be a site or not, and several such sites can share one domain. The panel refuses an address that could not work and prepares the main domain for its new neighbour by itself.

----------------------------------------
TOMORROW'S PHP TODAY, AND PHP PER PLATFORM
----------------------------------------

PHP 8.6 is still a release candidate, but where your server has it installed you can already point a copy of a site at it and see how your code will behave on the next PHP long before it becomes the norm; keep it to copies for now, since until its final release it runs without the Valkey cache and a few extensions such as Imagick. Each platform in your account can now run its command-line tools and panel tasks on its own PHP version, so an older and a newer codebase live happily side by side. And a task that would run a site's code on a PHP version it cannot handle now stops before it starts and tells you exactly why and what to change, instead of failing half way.

----------------------------------------
COPIES AND RESTORES THAT CHECK THE ROOM FIRST
----------------------------------------

Before a Clone, Migrate or Restore takes its safety copy, it measures everything it is about to copy and checks that the disk has room for all of it with a margin to spare. If it does not, the task stops right away with the numbers in its log and nothing is written. A copy that fails half way tidies up after itself and leaves the original site as it was, the automatic backup taken before a clone carries the site's database, and database triggers, stored procedures and scheduled events now travel with a site through every backup, copy and move. If one of your sites uses them, take a fresh backup now: archives made before this upgrade may lack them.

----------------------------------------
YOUR SITES STAY UP WHILE THE SERVER WORKS
----------------------------------------

Maintenance moments that used to cost your visitors a burst of errors no longer do: switching the server to a single PHP version, adding a PHP version, changing how your account assigns PHP to its sites, the routine password change of the cache service during an upgrade, all of them now keep your sites online. Drush keeps working on the PHP version chosen for you while an upgrade swaps in its fresh copy, and from the next upgrade on your panel is free for your own work sooner, because it no longer re-checks platforms that hold no sites.

----------------------------------------
DRUPAL 10 AND 11, SMOOTHER DAY TO DAY
----------------------------------------

Every Clone, Migrate and Restore of a Drupal or Backdrop site now applies its database updates, Drupal 10 and 11 included, and the old option to skip them is retired; the Run db updates task either completes with the site's own Drush or puts the site back as it was. Sites that use modern logging modules such as Monolog, and Drupal 11 sites with Webform, now verify, back up, clone and migrate without trouble. Your sites keep their hash salt through every Verify, Backup, Restore and Migrate, so login links and open forms keep working after maintenance, a new Drupal 11 site installs in the language you choose, and Composer can update Drupal core in your own codebases without tripping over the scaffold files.

----------------------------------------
ONE CHANGE OF POLICY WORTH KNOWING
----------------------------------------

The ready-made Drupal 8 and newer platforms BOA provides, every distribution and the plain Drupal 9, 10 and 11 cores, are now meant for demonstration and compatibility testing, with security updates on a best-effort basis. Production sites belong on a platform you build in your own account: if one of yours still runs on a ready-made platform, plan its move, and the guide at https://docs.boa.io/using/deploying-code/building-a-platform shows how.

----------------------------------------
DRUSH ON DRUPAL 8 AND NEWER: USE VDRUSH
----------------------------------------

On your Drupal 8 and newer sites, run Drush commands with vdrush, the site's own Drush. The older Drush 8 still answers uli, status, sql-dump and a few other read-only commands there; anything else now points you to vdrush instead of running a tool that does not fit the site.

Shell logins made for a single client now look after that client's files and themes only, while Drush, Composer and the database tools stay with your main login and the control panel. For a client's developer who needs those tools, your server administrator can now set up a platform developer login, with Drush and Composer on the platforms that hold only that client's sites.

----------------------------------------
A CONTROL PANEL THAT TAKES TURNS
----------------------------------------

Tasks in your panel now run one after another, so two tasks can never trip over the same site, and duplicate waiting tasks are folded into one. Every task shows which login started it, and a new site operator role lets a colleague run everyday site work without being able to back up, restore, delete or reset passwords. The one-time administrator login link of a new or reset site now appears on the site's own page, next to the house icon, for 24 hours, and big backup downloads from your panel now finish in the browser, however large.

----------------------------------------
FASTER, STEADIER PAGES FOR YOUR VISITORS
----------------------------------------

HTTP/3 connections now ride through the routine reloads every BOA server makes, so big uploads and downloads complete, and HTTP/3 moves large files as fast as HTTP/2. Audio and video play through to the end for listeners whose players read far ahead, AVIF images reach browsers as images and are cached like WebP, and when a crowd lands on a brand-new page at once, your visitors wait a moment for its first copy instead of all hitting PHP together: in our test, two page builds instead of seventy-one. A new shield turns away crawler networks that spread one scraper over hundreds of addresses, while real visitors pass as before.

Curious about the details? The full story of this release is at https://docs.boa.io/releases/boa-5-88-866
